A dead writer's hand, still writing in the steam.

Cook Mode for handwritten recipes. At the step where the writer left a judgement in their own words, steam condenses on the window over the pot and their scanned hand is wiped clear of the fog. The hand is real: one page of a c. 1750 receipt book in the Wellcome Collection. For anyone who would rather cook from, or check a transcription against, the writer's own hand than a retyped copy.

For the judges

Questions a judge would ask

Is the handwriting generated?

No. The ink is the scan's own ink lifted off the paper: its shape and density come from the scan and are drawn in the renderer's colour. scripts/verify_provenance.py re-fetches each note's region from the archive and checks that the bundled page crop matches it and that the bounding box of the displayed ink lies inside it. That is a region-level check, not a pixel-level proof: the displayed note is a processed render, and a pixel-level check was tried and dropped (docs/EXPERIMENTS.md, row 8).

Is that the writer's stroke order?

No. The write-on follows a traced path through that ink, so the order of strokes is ours, not the writer's. The README says so in its Solution section.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes, in emulation. A pane 640 px wide or narrower gets a phone layout that shows only the note: a line is cut at a gap in its ink when that keeps the hand readable or lets it grow, and the parts stack. The frame-rate runs used an emulated Pixel 5 with a 4× CPU throttle; no physical Android run was made.

What did Kiro actually do?

304 commits are labelled [kiro] (301 of them fall inside a recorded Kiro session's time window), made working through a spec's tasks, a bugfix spec or an agent workflow. Its two custom agents read the page. In our tests its hooks refused a write that adds a synthetic voice and a catalog write with no named reviewer; refusing a handwriting font is unit-tested. The shipped recipe's reviewer is the builder's agent. docs/KIRO.md lists each feature with the files and commits behind it, and what was planned and not built.

Where do the cooking times come from?

The manuscript gives no cooking times, so none is invented: each note is placed at its step or at a cited modern check time, and each time is labelled. In the shipped recipe all three notes arrive with their step.

What is not real yet?

Beyond the three limits above: the shipped recipe's sign-off came from the builder's agent, not a person, and a human sign-off is still owed; no build-wide Kiro credit total was kept. (The one end-to-end test that used to fail intermittently was a race in the test itself, now fixed: 114 of 114 pass.)

Still writing.
